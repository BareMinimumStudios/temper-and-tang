# Metals and equipment

## Start a workshop

Mine copper and tin with a stone pickaxe. Tin generates in new chunks. Smelt raw copper and raw tin in a furnace, then mix **3 copper ingots + 1 tin ingot** into four bronze blend and smelt it into bronze. This remains a simple route before you have tongs.

Make a charcoal hearth from cobblestone and coal or charcoal. Make the shaping anvil and smithing hammer with bronze. Tongs accept either the original iron recipe or the new bronze alternative: **two ingots, two matching nuggets and a stick**. This avoids an iron requirement before your first bronze equipment.

Keep tongs in either hand when carrying hot metal. Use the hearth's stock and fuel slots, then press **Space** or click the bellows. Leave the small additive dish empty to heat or refine; put an alloy ingredient in it to alloy. Closing returns your remaining ingredients. Fuel already burned is spent.

## Refine and alloy

| Process | Stock | Additive dish | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Refine | Raw copper, raw iron or raw tin | Empty | Hot ingots, using the installed smelting recipe |
| Bronze | 3 copper ingots | 1 tin ingot | 4 hot bronze ingots |
| Steel | 1 iron ingot | 1 coal or charcoal | 1 hot steel ingot |

The fuel slot is separate from the carbon ingredient. Steel consumes both. The station previews the result and fuel cost before starting. A batch may produce at most **8 ingots**. Use 3 or 6 copper for bronze; surplus additive is returned. Steel accepts up to 8 iron with matching carbon. Unsupported or incomplete batches cannot start.

Refining follows existing furnace recipes; removing a smelting recipe also removes that hearth route. The hearth grants hot stock rather than furnace experience. A furnace remains a valid refining alternative. Heating does not improve the grade of finished equipment.

## Choose equipment

**Bronze → iron → steel** is the straightforward metal progression. Tin is an alloy ingredient, not a gear tier. Bronze tools can mine iron and tin but cannot mine diamond. Steel tools have iron mining capability, greater speed and durability; they cannot mine obsidian.

Bronze and steel each have a sword, pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, four armor pieces, dagger, spear and warhammer. Iron uses vanilla tools and armor plus the new dagger, spear and warhammer. Daggers trade power for speed; spears strike more slowly and gain one block of melee interaction reach; warhammers strike harder and more slowly. Better Combat profiles are supplied when that optional mod is installed.

Bronze armor uses the familiar gold appearance; steel uses iron. Their stats and repair materials belong to Temper & Tang. Armor accepts vanilla trims and enchantments. Common material tags allow compatible ingots from other mods.

## Make and finish gear

Heat the ingredients, place them in the shaping anvil's pattern, and select the desired result. A spear uses a diagonal pattern rather than the shovel's vertical pattern. Follow the moving rhythm notes and strike as they reach the fixed green target. Harder metals need greater precision. Accuracy, mastery and a bounded random roll determine craftsmanship.

Quench the shaped blank in water before using it. Finish the completed equipment at the finishing anvil for a smaller final adjustment. Hold **Shift** for material, trait and workmanship details; **H** opens station help. Advanced optional-mod patterns use the existing reusable blueprint system.

Simply Swords and Epic Knights keep their own gear identities, ingredients and baseline stats. Existing recipes removed by the pack stay removed. Shared tags allow workshop steel and nuggets to feed Epic Knights components; they do not automatically merge existing stacks. Pack-wide output preferences remain the job of an ingredient-unification mod.
